SA2Real: Translating Shared Autonomy to the Real World

Proposal in preparation. Date and venue to be confirmed.

Shared autonomy (SA) combines user input with robot assistance. Although research in SA continues to progress, moving these systems from controlled laboratory settings to real-world use is still difficult. Systems may need capabilities that were not required in the settings they were developed for.

Assumptions are important in research as they help simplify problems and focus on specific questions. However, when methods are used beyond their original settings, some assumptions can limit what users are able to do in practice.

This workshop aims to bring together researchers and practitioners from academia and industry working across different application domains. Through invited talks and a panel discussion, participants will share their experiences to help us understand which capabilities SA systems need in practice, how these needs differ across applications, and which assumptions may restrict them.

Building on these discussions, a rotating group activity will allow participants to examine proposed capabilities and suggest ways to develop and evaluate them. Contributions will be shared on this website for others to use in their work.

Topics of interest

  • Capabilities needed for SA systems in real-world use.
  • Capabilities that transfer across domains and requirements that remain application specific.
  • Assumptions about users, tasks and environments, robots, and human–robot interaction.
  • Research directions and system improvements that address capability limitations.
  • Evaluation approaches and evidence needed beyond controlled laboratory settings.

We welcome participants interested in SA across all tasks, robot platforms, and application domains. See Get involved for participation details.
