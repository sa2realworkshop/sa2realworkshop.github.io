Schedule

SA2Real is planned as an interactive half-day workshop combining perspectives from invited speakers with participant-led discussions. The programme will move from application-specific experiences to group exploration, cross-domain reflection, and a final panel discussion on future directions for real-world shared autonomy.

This schedule is provisional. The workshop date and time zone will be confirmed.

Application primers

Invited speakers will introduce challenges, experiences, and lessons from different application domains, including assistive robotics, manufacturing, hazardous environments, and field robotics. These short primers will provide practical context and starting points for the discussions that follow.

Group activity

Participants will be divided into four to five groups, depending on attendance. Each group will select an application domain and consider how a shared autonomy system could be deployed in a real-world use case within that domain. Where possible, invited speakers will join groups related to their expertise and contribute practical perspectives to the discussion.

Groups will consider topics including:

  1. Capabilities and design goals needed for real-world shared autonomy.
  2. Assumptions about users, robots, tasks, and environments, whether they remain valid in real-world settings, and how they may affect users and system performance.
  3. Academic and industry practices for developing, evaluating, and deploying shared autonomy systems.
  4. Experiences and lessons from platforms such as manipulators, drones, mobile robots, and multi-robot systems.
  5. Open questions, research gaps, and possible directions for developing and evaluating real-world shared autonomy systems.

Each group will capture its key ideas in a shared slide deck using a provided template. These slides will support comparison across groups and contribute to the workshop's shared outcomes.

Poster session

Accepted submissions will be presented as posters during the coffee break. The session will provide an informal setting for participants to share ongoing work, exchange feedback, and connect with researchers and practitioners working on shared autonomy across different domains.

Between-group reflections

Each group will briefly present the key ideas captured in its slides. Participants from other groups will be able to add comments and perspectives directly to the shared slide deck, followed by a brief Q&A.

The aim is to identify which capabilities, assumptions, and challenges are shared across domains and which are specific to particular applications. Laptops will be provided by the organizers if needed.

Panel discussion

The invited speakers will reconvene for a final panel discussion drawing on themes and open questions emerging throughout the workshop. The discussion will focus on promising research directions, practical challenges, and what may be needed to move shared autonomy systems toward broader real-world use.
